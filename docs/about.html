About BendLab

BendLab asks a simple question with a measurable answer: when a sunflower seedling bends toward light, which of its internal settings does the bending curve actually reveal, and which stay hidden?

The question

We model a seedling with four settings: sensitivity (k), response lag (tau), a bend-rate cap (max_rate), and an effective light angle (phi0). If two very different settings produce the same curve, the curve cannot tell them apart, and any method that claims to read them off, from curve fitting to a neural network, is guessing. BendLab measures exactly where that information limit sits. It is a model-identifiability study, not yet evidence for a particular biological mechanism.

Key results

  • Degeneracy is real: different parameter combinations produce nearly identical bending curves.
  • One dial is blind: when the bend-rate cap never binds, max_rate cannot be recovered. Curve fits flag it in 47 of 48 measured plants; a 400,000-plant sweep and a trained neural network (about 44% error, versus 1 to 4% for the other dials) agree.
  • Fitting is not predicting: the model fits published curves well in-sample (median 1.80 degrees on PLOS Biology S13 Fig1C) but predicts held-out stimulus-onset groups weakly (11.75 degrees).
  • External benchmark (Phase B0, n = 268): on independent unilateral-light trajectories across 10 intensities (Kempinski et al. 2026), fixing phi = 90° overshoots (14.41° held-out median RMSE vs 9.73° group-mean baseline) because phi0 in the minimal model acts as an empirical equilibrium angle (measured plateaus 18–37°; per-plant phi_free median 28.8°) rather than physical lamp geometry; the W2 effective-angle diagnostic reaches 9.97°.
  • Shape & growth extensions: observing full multi-segment stem curvature reduces k twin-lookup error by 33–49% (46.8% on a leakage-safe split, while max_rate stays unidentifiable); starting hypocotyl length L0 (n = 213) is associated with faster 0–2 h bending (+2.12 °/h per log unit, 95% CI [0.66, 3.62]) without improving held-out ODE trajectory RMSE over M0 (10.25°, all variants within 1.5%).

Limitations, in plain sight

  • The neural model has never seen a real plant. Fitted sunflower parameters often lie outside its training ranges, so do not apply it to measured curves without checking the self-consistency banner.
  • The canonical four-parameter model treats phi0 as an effective equilibrium angle under static light; physical lamp geometry requires explicit straightening (gamma) or circadian modulation (sim_clock_gamma.py).
  • Several earlier conclusions were corrected or withdrawn after audit (the 13 Oct fitter time-base error, the H3 and H8 verdicts, the H6 connectivity claim, and Workstream B feature indexing). Every correction is documented in the repository.
  • Nothing here is a validated wet-lab protocol. Sample-size and protocol-discrimination estimates come from stated simulation procedures and await laboratory testing.
